Added proxies affect spring endpoints through the existing integrator. They do not change authored spring stiffness, gravity, drag, joint lists or imported collider resources. Humanoid chains and the same arm's descendant sleeve chains are excluded. A weak reference from each callback to the owner avoids a retained callback ownership cycle.

The updated independent actual-rig check passed **2,224 assertions, zero failures**, across all five published rigs: default loading leaves extra contacts disabled with zero proxies; explicit experimental shallow contact corrects a one-degree intrusion while deep overlap yields; storage-property snapshots before/after configuration and cleanup, repeated configuration without duplicate proxies, exact preservation of pre-existing collider counts, clear/reload detachment, safe retained callbacks after detach, humanoid exclusion, and the real collider callback at uniform scale 0.6/1/1.4 with yaw 45° and translation. ## Behavior evidence

The initial three-rig × two-motion manual A/B comparison is withdrawn as runtime-improvement evidence. With the normal SkeletonModifier enabled, Godot preserves the premodifier pose, uploads the modified skin, and restores that input pose. `VrmAvatar.apply_pose()` resets only humanoid mapped bones; the inactive-modifier probe left nonhumanoid bone transforms from the prior physics tick in place. Resetting the Verlet positions would also be wrong: those positions are the intended dynamic state. A corrected controlled test must restore the full input bone transforms while preserving the Verlet state, and confirm its behavior against the actual enabled modifier. See the [official SkeletonModifier design](https://godotengine.org/article/design-of-the-skeleton-modifier-3d/).

An independent equivalence probe now confirms the correction: Rice and Eishin × walk and sit, 1,438 actual enabled-modifier callbacks, produced exactly identical spring tail positions and every bone global transform component in a separate manual avatar when its full input pose was restored before each manual tick. `review-modifier-equivalence.json`, log, source and hashes retain the result. Reproduce with `--headless --fixed-fps 60 --path native --script ../diagnostics/physics_cloth/review_modifier_equivalence.gd`. There were approximately two actual modifier callbacks per process-frame iteration in this test, so a separate one-tick-per-iteration controlled probe is not evidence of identical wall-time runtime motion. The first equivalence attempt reset only once per process iteration and failed; its log is retained. This correction validates the per-tick input-state model, not the appearance of the resulting garment.

A second independent control isolated a translated-pair measurement confound. The sparse sampler's bind indices, vertex/joint/weight arrays, selected vertices and triangles match exactly across separately imported twins for all five rigs, and prephysics sampled positions match exactly. However, twins at ±0.56 m with **identical authored physics and no contact difference** reproduce the initial quality matrix's large maxima (Cheval 1.622544 m, Rice 1.433819 m, Eishin 1.267444 m). At the same origin, all five rigs match exactly over 60 identical ticks. Thus those translated-pair maxima cannot be attributed to new contacts. Reports and the independent control are `review-sample-correspondence-before-rebase.json` and `review-sample-correspondence-origin.json` and `review_sample_correspondence.gd`. This control additionally motivates inspection of existing spring initialization coordinate conventions; it does not assert that the ordinary same-origin authored physics has no startup deformation.

The earlier Eishin seated captures show substantial garment deflection and asymmetric hem changes. A finite tail and zero proxy endpoint overlap alone do not establish that the garment remains well shaped. Pending acceptance includes actual modified-skin silhouettes, garment deformation/edge stretch and bone lengths, temporal discontinuities and settling, using identical source motion in the comparison. The original artifact and initial review are retained as historical records, not approved runtime evidence.

The mini authoring helper was independently exercised against both published mini VRMs: existing secondary metadata remains byte-equivalent as parsed data; first authoring on a copy with empty secondary metadata changes only `secondaryAnimation`; a second call is idempotent. Results are in `review-mini-authoring.json`. The published mini displayed-frame probe has 20/17 imported spring chains and 120/102 added contacts for Mambo/Hachimi, respectively. Both remain finite over 360 sampled displayed frames; maximum sampled tail steps are approximately 5.95/8.45 mm. The helper explicitly identifies this as new Mate tuning, not recovered game physics. These measurements do not establish arbitrary-motion stability or cloth wrinkles.

## Rebase correction and final bounded appearance review

The new `VrmAvatar.rebase_secondary_physics()` seeds current/previous tails from the final posed bone tip in the same forward-center coordinates and fixed-length convention as the integrator. Independently checked **1,080 assertions, zero failures** on all five rigs, at uniform scale 0.6/1/1.4 with yaw, translation and a posed head. Repeated rebasing is idempotent, velocity starts at zero, and resource/state/joint/length identities remain unchanged. Actual seated-floor constraints retain 14/24/19/20/17 colliders respectively, including their terminal joints. Segment-length error is below 0.00000012 m. `review-rebase.json`, its log and source retain this run.

Repeating the original translated-twin same-physics control after the fix reduces every rig's maximum sampled surface difference from approximately 0.9–1.6 m to below 0.00000185 m over 60 ticks. This independent behavioral control supports the initialization fix beyond a formula-mirroring assertion. `review-sample-correspondence.json` retains the corrected run; its before-rebase counterpart is preserved.

The root's two presentation hooks were inspected: successful startup/character/variant loading all reach `_frame_avatar()`, which rebases after final pivot placement; explicit view settings/reset rebase after object view refresh. No normal per-frame or walking reset is present in these hooks. They handle discontinuous presentation setup without suppressing ongoing ordinary motion's inertia.

Inspected the final same-origin `quality_revision/shape-rebased.json`, the corrected sparse-skin probe, mini initial renders and sampled walking/settling sequences. Mambo/Hachimi preserve their recognizable hairstyle and costume outline in the inspected samples; the initial inward hair collapse is absent. Rest maximum sampled displacement is 9.6/17.6 mm; sampled edge-change p95 remains below 0.8%. Across their six rest/walk/sit cells, segment-length error stays below 0.00000023 m, sampled normals do not reverse, and final-second settling steps remain below 0.000003 m. This supports the bounded default authored-spring behavior after initialization correction.

The front-camera render samples are 5 Hz; dynamics metrics are measured each 60 Hz tick. This is not all-angle, all-triangle or arbitrary-motion acceptance. Hachimi's hair moves substantially during walking, and the worst sampled edge changes reach 19–38% during walking despite p95 staying below 1.8%; these weighted spring bones are not inextensible cloth. The explicit source-pose reset halfway through each clip is separated from ordinary motion. Original-character optional contact experiments still show sizeable local walking deformations and remain disabled.

## Cloth experiment and limitations

Inspected the Jolt probe source, measured JSON and actual rendered proxy. It simulates a newly constructed 288-vertex skirt with 32 pinned waist vertices and moving collision bodies. It does not modify a character costume or switch the production pet to cloth simulation. Its finite deformation and pinned-point error support a native-engine feasibility result.

The 3.51 ms mean physics-process monitor versus 1.09 ms preceding baseline is an exploratory sequential scene observation. The active cloth begins immediately after creation, while the baseline is warmed; phase and sampling duration also differ. This is not an isolated steady-state cloth cost. Physics runs on the CPU; using the RTX 4090 renderer does not imply GPU cloth simulation. GPU timing returned zero and is unavailable. The existing README accurately limits production performance and garment claims.

No blocker remains for the reviewed default-off contact policy, rebase initialization, bounded mini authored-spring behavior, and separately scoped Jolt feasibility experiment. Production enablement of experimental hand contacts remains unapproved. Garment-specific simulation meshes, skin-transfer bindings, body/furniture collision coverage and fine wrinkle topology remain separate implementation work. Exact reviewed source and asset hashes are retained in `review-source-hashes.json`; no Windows application was launched by this reviewer.
